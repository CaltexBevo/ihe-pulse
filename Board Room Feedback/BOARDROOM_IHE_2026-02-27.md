## 🔷 LEADER 01: CPV — Chief Product Visionary
### DNA: Jobs × Bezos × Cagan × Spolsky × Chesky

### Product Vision Assessment

**The Core Question:** Is InnovatingHigherEd a content platform, a community, a product company, or a media brand? Right now, it's trying to be all four simultaneously, and that ambiguity is the single biggest strategic risk.

**What the CPV Sees:**

IHE has strong product-market intuition. The Innovation Pulse — a daily curated AI briefing specifically for higher ed — is a genuinely differentiated content product. Nobody else owns this. Inside Higher Ed covers broad news. Enrollify focuses on enrollment marketing. EdSurge tilts K-12. The "daily AI briefing for higher ed practitioners" category is unclaimed, and IHE has planted its flag. That's a Jobs-level instinct for an underserved need.

The platform architecture on the refresh site is well-conceived. Six content pillars (Innovation Pulse, Podcast, AI Directory, Prompts, Tinker Lab, Educator Tools) each serve a distinct user job-to-be-done. The Innovation Pulse is "keep me informed daily." The Podcast is "go deep on a topic." The AI Directory is "help me find the right tool." The Prompt Navigator is "give me something I can use Monday morning." The Tinker Lab is "show me what's possible." This is a thoughtful content ecosystem, not a random collection of pages.

**Where the CPV Pushes Back (Category A — Structural):**

1. **The product vision is diffuse.** The strategy docs mention premium memberships, online courses, consulting, webinars, community forums, paid newsletters, a virtual summit, book sales, affiliate marketing, and institutional licenses — all as near-term possibilities. This is a two-person team with zero budget. Trying to build even three of these simultaneously will produce mediocre versions of all of them. The Bezos framework demands: what is the ONE thing that, if you nail it, makes everything else easier or unnecessary?

2. **"Educator Tools" is a zombie section.** It overlaps with the AI Directory but isn't clearly differentiated. Is it tools built BY educators, or tools FOR educators? If the former, how is it curated? If the latter, how does it differ from the AI Directory? Kill it or merge it. Seven nav items is one too many.

3. **The platform doesn't have a product.** IHE currently produces content. Content is valuable but it's not a product until someone pays for it. The strategy docs assume a progression from free content → freemium membership → courses → consulting, but there's no concrete product definition for any of these tiers. What exactly does "InnovatingHigherEd Plus" include that's worth paying for? Until you can answer that in one sentence, don't build it.

**Where the CPV Builds (Category B — Visionary Opportunity):**

The Innovation Pulse IS the product. It's not a feature of the website — it should BE the business model. Think of it as IHE's version of Morning Brew, The Hustle, or The Skimm — but for higher ed AI. Those companies were acquired for $75M+ (Morning Brew by Business Insider) and $20M+ (The Skimm, investment rounds) on the back of a single daily email with a distinctive editorial voice.

The Pulse already has the right bones: daily cadence, curated stories, editorial perspective ("Connecting the Dots"), audio companion, and a clear audience. The monetization path isn't memberships or courses — it's the newsletter itself. Grow the list, prove engagement, monetize through sponsored content and eventually premium tiers.

**CPV Verdict:** The platform is strategically sound but operationally overextended. Narrow the focus. The Innovation Pulse is the product. Everything else is supporting content.

**CPV Priority Actions:**
1. Declare the Innovation Pulse as the primary product, not a feature
2. Kill or merge Educator Tools into AI Directory
3. Defer all premium membership/course/community plans until newsletter hits 5,000+
4. Define the "one metric that matters" for the next 90 days: newsletter subscriber growth

Even capturing 1-2% mindshare in the "AI-forward faculty" segment (estimated at 15-20% of total faculty, or 225,000-300,000 people) represents a meaningful audience.

## 🔷 LEADER 03: HUR — Head of User Research
### DNA: Erika Hall × Steve Portigal × Indi Young × Teresa Torres × Kim Goodwin

### User Research Assessment

**The User Problem is Real But Underspecified:**

The strategy docs correctly identify the core pain: faculty feel overwhelmed by AI, need practical guidance, and lack institutional support. But the Founder's Brief lists the target audience as "faculty, instructional designers, department chairs, deans, CIOs, IT leaders, and edtech innovators." That's not a target audience — that's the entire higher education ecosystem.

**The HUR's Primary Concern (Category A — Structural):**

Each of these user segments has fundamentally different needs, different content consumption patterns, and different willingness to pay:

- **Faculty (the practitioner):** "Tell me what AI tool to use in my intro comp class on Tuesday." Needs immediate, practical, classroom-ready guidance. Consumes content in 5-minute bursts between classes. Rarely pays out-of-pocket for professional development.

- **Instructional Designers:** "Help me redesign this course sequence to integrate AI across 15 sections." Needs systematic frameworks, not tips. Consumes long-form content and workshops. Their institution pays.

- **Deans / Department Chairs:** "How do I create an AI adoption strategy for my department without alienating technophobic faculty?" Needs political navigation guidance and peer case studies. Consumes reports and case studies. Institution pays.

- **CIOs:** "What AI infrastructure investments should we make this fiscal year?" Needs data, benchmarks, ROI projections. Consumes formal reports and executive briefings. Very different content than what a faculty member needs.

**Trying to serve all four with the same content is how you end up serving none of them well.** The Innovation Pulse can cast a wide net (news is relevant to all), but the deeper content (prompts, directory, workshops) should be laser-focused on ONE primary persona.

**The HUR's Recommendation:**

Based on Dr. Jones's domain expertise, institutional access, and existing content, the primary persona should be: **Community college faculty who are AI-curious but not AI-expert, teaching introductory courses, with limited institutional AI support.** This is the persona Dr. Jones knows intimately, has direct access to, and can speak to with authentic credibility.

Everyone else is secondary. Deans, CIOs, and instructional designers can benefit from the Innovation Pulse and podcast, but the core product experience should be designed for the practitioner faculty member.

**User Journey Gap Analysis:**

The refresh site is missing a clear user journey. A first-time visitor lands on the homepage and sees: a daily briefing, top stories, podcast episodes, AI tools, prompts, and a tinker lab. There's no guided path. No "start here" experience. No segmentation.

**Recommended user journey:**
1. **Discovery:** Faculty member hears about IHE from a colleague, conference, or social media
2. **First Visit:** Homepage → Innovation Pulse catches their attention → They read today's briefing
3. **Hook:** "Never Miss a Pulse" newsletter signup (bottom of the page is too late — should be above the fold or as a modal after 30 seconds)
4. **Habit Formation:** Daily email → they open it 3-4 times per week → IHE becomes part of their routine
5. **Deepening:** They explore the AI Directory for a specific tool → find a prompt template → try the Tinker Lab
6. **Engagement:** They submit a "Be Our Guest" application, or suggest a tool for the directory
7. **Conversion:** When workshops/consulting launch, they're the first to register (or recommend to their dean)

Currently, steps 3-7 have friction or don't exist.

**Missing Research the HUR Demands:**

Where are the user interviews? The OER platform had Dr. Jones's direct validation of faculty pain points. IHE has strategy documents with assumptions about what educators need, but no documented conversations with actual users of the current site. **The HUR recommends 10 brief interviews with current newsletter subscribers** to validate: what content do they actually read? What do they skip? What's missing? What would they pay for?

**HUR Verdict:** The user problem is real but the audience is too broad. Narrow to community college faculty as the primary persona. Conduct user interviews before building any premium features.

**HUR Priority Actions:**
1. Define a single primary persona (recommended: community college AI-curious faculty)
2. Add a "Start Here" or "New to AI?" guided path on the site
3. Move newsletter signup higher on the homepage — above the fold or as an early-scroll prompt
4. Conduct 10 interviews with current subscribers to validate assumptions
5. Add a simple onboarding question to newsletter signup: "What's your role?" (Faculty / Designer / Admin / Other) — this data is gold for segmentation later

## 🔷 LEADER 04: CDO — Chief Design Officer
### DNA: Jony Ive × Dieter Rams × Julie Zhuo × John Maeda × Mike Monteiro

### Design Evaluation of the Refresh Site (ihe-pulse.vercel.app)

**Overall Impression:**

The refresh is a significant upgrade from a typical WordPress site. The information architecture is logical, the content hierarchy is clear, and the visual language is consistent. This is a professional-looking platform. But the CDO has concerns.

**What's Working (Rams Principle: Good design is as little design as possible):**

1. **The Innovation Pulse layout is excellent.** The quote-of-the-day block, audio player with waveform visualization, date-stamped daily editions, and the sidebar "In This Issue" table of contents — this is thoughtful editorial design that communicates "we take this seriously."

2. **Content categorization is clear.** Tags like "Lead Story," "Top Story," "Insights & Trends," "Case Study," and skill-level badges on prompts (Beginner, Intermediate) help users orient quickly.

3. **The AI Directory cards are well-structured.** Tool name, category, pricing, description, and editorial badges (Trending, Staff Pick, New) give users what they need at a glance.

4. **The About page "What We Stand For" section** (Innovation With Purpose, Practical Over Theoretical, Community-Driven, Equity at the Center) is a strong values statement that builds trust.

**Where the CDO Pushes Back Hard (Category A — Structural Design Problems):**

1. **The dark theme is a potential alienation risk.** This is the CDO's most significant concern. The target audience is predominantly 35-65 year old higher education professionals — many of whom work in institutional environments with fluorescent lighting, access content during work hours, and associate dark themes with gaming or developer culture, not academic resources. The dark cyberpunk aesthetic with cyan/magenta accents reads as "tech startup" or "developer tool," not "trusted academic resource."

   This doesn't mean dark themes can't work. But the CDO notes that EVERY major competitor in the space uses light themes: Inside Higher Ed, EdSurge, Enrollify, Teaching in Higher Ed, Educause, Faculty Focus. There's a reason for that — their audiences are comfortable with it. Going dark is a bold choice. **The CDO recommends A/B testing this or at minimum offering a light mode toggle.** If analytics show high bounce rates from first-time visitors, the theme is the first suspect.

2. **No photos of the founders on the About page.** The bio cards are text-only. In a trust-driven space where Dr. Jones's personal credibility IS the brand, this is a critical omission. Faculty trust people they can see. A professional headshot of Dr. Jones should be the most prominent visual element on the About page. This is not optional — it's foundational to the brand's credibility proposition.

3. **The homepage is long but lacks a clear call to action above the fold.** The first screen shows the Innovation Pulse quote and audio player, which is good. But the newsletter signup ("Never Miss a Pulse") is at the very bottom of the page, past five content sections. A user has to scroll through stories, podcast, AI directory, prompts, AND tinker lab before they hit the conversion point. The Maeda simplicity principle says: what's the ONE thing you want a first-time visitor to do? If it's subscribe, that CTA needs to appear in the first viewport.

4. **The Tinker Lab section shows only two items.** This makes the section feel sparse and undermines the "active experimentation" positioning. Either produce more content before launching, or remove the section from the homepage and link to it only from the nav until there's enough content to fill it.

5. **No social proof anywhere on the homepage.** No testimonials, no "trusted by faculty at" logos, no podcast download numbers, no guest endorsements. The "1,200+ educators" on the newsletter CTA is the only social proof element. For a platform that depends on peer trust, this is a significant design gap.

**CDO Verdict:** The refresh is a strong upgrade with real design quality. The dark theme and missing social proof are the two biggest risks. Address founder photos and newsletter CTA placement before migration.

**CDO Priority Actions:**
1. Add Dr. Jones headshot to About page immediately — non-negotiable
2. Add newsletter signup CTA above the fold on homepage (can be subtle — a slim banner or inline prompt)
3. Add 3-5 social proof elements: podcast guest quotes, subscriber testimonials, university name-drops
4. Implement light mode toggle or seriously consider A/B testing the theme
5. Consider adding a "Start Here" or "New to IHE?" entry point for first-time visitors

## 🔷 LEADER 05: CTO — Chief Technology Architect
### DNA: Werner Vogels × Martin Fowler × Kelsey Hightower × Charity Majors × Gene Kim

### Technical Architecture Assessment

**Stack Assessment:**

The refresh runs on Next.js deployed to Vercel. This is a strong architectural choice for a content-heavy platform. Next.js provides server-side rendering for SEO (critical for discoverability), static generation for performance, and a React component model for interactive features. Vercel's deployment pipeline is zero-configuration and includes edge caching, preview deployments, and analytics.

**What the CTO Approves:**

1. **Next.js is the right framework for this use case.** Content sites need SEO, speed, and the ability to add dynamic features incrementally. Next.js delivers all three. The WordPress-to-Next.js migration is the right move.

2. **Vercel hosting is appropriate for current scale.** For a content site with <10,000 daily visitors, Vercel's free/hobby tier is sufficient. The auto-scaling and edge network mean the site will handle traffic spikes (e.g., if a podcast episode goes viral) without infrastructure management.

3. **The content structure appears to support future API-driven features.** The AI Directory, Prompt Navigator, and Innovation Pulse all look like they could be backed by a CMS or database, which would enable filtering, search, user submissions, and eventually user accounts.

**Where the CTO Pushes Back:**

1. **What's the content management story?** Is Brent manually editing Next.js files to publish each Innovation Pulse edition? Or is there a headless CMS (Sanity, Contentful, Strapi) behind this? If it's manual code deployments for daily content, that's unsustainable. **A two-person team publishing daily needs a CMS that Dr. Jones could use without touching code.** This is the CTO's #1 concern.

2. **What's the email infrastructure?** The "Never Miss a Pulse" newsletter is the core product (per CPV's assessment), but what's sending it? Mailchimp? ConvertKit? Beehiiv? Substack? The choice matters enormously for growth, monetization, and analytics. Beehiiv or ConvertKit would be optimal for a newsletter-first strategy — they're built for exactly this use case, with built-in referral programs, sponsorship marketplaces, and subscriber analytics.

3. **Search and filtering will become critical.** With 39+ tools in the AI Directory and 24+ prompts, users already need better discovery than browsing cards. As these libraries grow to 100+, search, filtering by category/use-case/price, and sorting become essential. Plan for this now in the data model.

4. **Analytics instrumentation.** Is there any analytics beyond Vercel's built-in? The HMI flagged the need for engagement metrics. At minimum: Google Analytics 4 or Plausible/Fathom for page-level analytics, plus newsletter-level analytics (open rates, click rates by edition/story). Without data, every decision is a guess.

5. **The .vercel.app staging URL needs proper domain setup.** Before migration, ensure innovatinghighered.com DNS is configured for Vercel with proper SSL, redirect rules from old WordPress URL structures, and sitemap submission to Google Search Console. Losing SEO juice during migration is a common and costly mistake.

**Scalability Roadmap:**

| Phase | Tech Need | Recommendation |
|-------|-----------|---------------|
| Now | CMS for daily publishing | Sanity or Contentful (free tier sufficient) |
| Now | Email platform | Beehiiv or ConvertKit |
| Now | Analytics | Plausible + newsletter analytics |
| Q2 2026 | Search/filtering in Directory | Algolia or built-in Next.js search |
| Q3 2026 | User accounts (for premium) | NextAuth.js + Supabase or Firebase |
| Q4 2026 | Payment processing | Stripe integration |
| 2027 | Community features | Might outsource to Circle or Discord rather than build |

**CTO Verdict:** The technical foundation is sound. The critical gap is the content management and email infrastructure — these must be production-ready before migration, not after.

**CTO Priority Actions:**
1. Confirm CMS strategy — daily publishing cannot depend on code deployments
2. Select and configure email platform (Beehiiv recommended for newsletter-first strategy)
3. Set up proper domain migration with redirects from all old WordPress URLs
4. Instrument analytics (page-level + newsletter-level)
5. Submit sitemap to Google Search Console post-migration

## 🔷 LEADER 08: CMO — Chief Marketing Officer
### DNA: Edward Bernays × Seth Godin × Binet & Field × April Dunford × Emily Heyward

### Brand & Positioning Assessment

**Current Positioning:**

IHE positions itself as "AI Innovation for Education" — empowering educators to navigate the AI landscape. This is accurate but generic. It could describe any of the six competitors listed in the Founder's Brief.

**The CMO's Reposition:**

The positioning needs to be sharper. Here's what IHE actually IS, distilled through the Dunford "Obviously Awesome" framework:

- **Competitive category:** Daily AI intelligence for higher education (NOT "edtech blog" or "podcast")
- **Differentiated value:** The only daily curated briefing connecting AI developments to specific higher ed practice implications, edited by an active faculty member
- **Target customer:** AI-curious faculty at community colleges and regional universities who don't have institutional AI support teams
- **Unique credibility:** Dr. Jones teaches, trains, and uses these tools in her own classroom — she's not a journalist, analyst, or vendor. She's a practitioner who reports from the field.

**Proposed positioning statement:** "InnovatingHigherEd is the daily AI briefing built by educators, for educators — connecting what's happening in AI to what matters in your classroom."

This is cleaner than "AI Innovation for Education" because it's specific about the format (daily briefing), the creator (educators), the audience (educators), and the value (connecting AI to classroom practice).

**Brand Architecture Across the Jones Portfolio:**

| Platform | Role | Audience |
|----------|------|----------|
| InnovatingHigherEd | The daily briefing + community | Faculty & instructional designers |
| AI College Rankings | The diagnostic scorecard | Students, parents, institutional leaders |
| AI OER Platform | The product/tool | Faculty adopting OER |
| Vibe Presenter | The classroom tool | Faculty in live teaching |

These should be perceived as related but independent brands. InnovatingHigherEd is the editorial/community brand. The others are product brands. Cross-linking is smart; co-branding is risky (it dilutes IHE's editorial independence).

**Content Marketing Strategy:**

## 🔷 LEADER 11: HCS — Head of Customer Success
### DNA: Nick Mehta × Dan Steinman × Lincoln Murphy × Donna Weber × Mary Poppen

### Customer Journey Assessment

**Who is the "customer" for IHE?**

At this stage, IHE has users (newsletter subscribers, website visitors, podcast listeners) not paying customers. The HCS frames the customer journey as: how does a faculty member go from "never heard of IHE" to "evangelizes IHE to their entire department"?

**Customer Journey Map:**

**Stage 1: Awareness** (How they find IHE)
- Hears Dr. Jones speak at a conference
- Colleague forwards the Innovation Pulse
- Finds the AI Directory via Google search
- Sees Dr. Jones's LinkedIn post
- Discovers podcast via Apple/Spotify

**Stage 2: First Value** (What makes them stay)
- Reads one Innovation Pulse edition and thinks "this is useful"
- Finds a specific AI tool in the Directory they didn't know about
- Copies a prompt from the Prompt Navigator and uses it in class
- Listens to a podcast episode and relates to the guest's experience

**Stage 3: Habit Formation** (What makes it stick)
- Subscribes to the newsletter
- Opens the Pulse 3+ times per week
- Bookmarks the AI Directory
- Listens to 3+ podcast episodes

**Stage 4: Engagement** (What turns them from consumer to participant)
- Submits a "Be Our Guest" application
- Replies to a newsletter edition with feedback
- Suggests a tool for the directory
- Shares the Pulse with a colleague

**Stage 5: Advocacy** (What makes them an evangelist)
- Recommends IHE in a department meeting
- Invites Dr. Jones to speak at their institution
- Signs their department up for a workshop
- Becomes a repeat workshop attendee

**Where the Journey Breaks:**

- **Awareness → First Value:** Currently depends too much on organic discovery. There's no structured onboarding for first-time visitors. No "Start Here" path. No welcome email sequence for new subscribers.
- **First Value → Habit:** The daily Pulse email is the best habit mechanism, but there's no engagement tracking to identify at-risk subscribers (those who stop opening).
- **Habit → Engagement:** There are no clear "next steps" for engaged readers. No prompts to interact, no community to join, no feedback mechanisms beyond the newsletter.
- **Engagement → Advocacy:** There's no way for enthusiastic readers to bring IHE to their institutions. No "Bring IHE to Your Campus" page, no workshop inquiry form, no departmental recommendations.

**HCS Recommended Onboarding Sequence:**

For new newsletter subscribers:
1. **Welcome email (Day 0):** "Welcome to the Innovation Pulse — here's what to expect"
2. **Value email (Day 2):** "Here are our 3 most popular resources" (top prompt, top tool, best podcast episode)
3. **Engagement email (Day 7):** "What's your biggest AI question? Reply and tell us"
4. **Social email (Day 14):** "Know a colleague who'd find this useful? Forward this email"

This simple 4-email sequence turns passive subscribers into engaged readers.

**HCS Verdict:** The customer journey exists organically but isn't designed or optimized. The biggest gaps are: no onboarding sequence, no engagement tracking, and no path from individual reader to institutional customer.

**HCS Priority Actions:**
1. Build a 4-email welcome sequence for new subscribers
2. Create a "Bring IHE to Your Campus" page for workshop inquiries
3. Add a "Reply with your biggest AI question" prompt to monthly Pulse editions
4. Track subscriber engagement tiers: active (opens 3+/week), engaged (opens 1-2/week), at-risk (hasn't opened in 2 weeks), churned (hasn't opened in 4 weeks)
