## STORIES

### LEAD STORY: Insights & Trends

**Headline:** Los Angeles Community Colleges Spend $3.8M on Chatbots That Give Students Wrong Answers

**Source:** LAist/CalMatters
**URL:** https://laist.com/news/education/california-colleges-spend-millions-on-faulty-ai-systems-the-chatbot-is-outdated

**Summary:**
The Los Angeles Community College District has spent $3.8 million on A.I. chatbots since 2021, yet students report the systems frequently provide outdated or inaccurate information. CalMatters and LAist found that the chatbots answer general questions correctly but struggle with specifics.

At East Los Angeles College, the bot incorrectly identified the college president, naming Alberto Román, who had left the position to become the district's chancellor. The same system provided wrong financial aid office hours and misdirected Spanish-speaking users.

Students describe the chatbots as unreliable. Pablo Aguirre, an I.T. intern at East Los Angeles College, said he abandoned the chatbot for Google, Reddit, and the college website. Reanna Carlson, a commercial music major at Fresno City College, stated that the chatbot "is outdated and can't navigate the services we provide on campus effectively." Bryan Hartanto, an international student at Santa Monica College from Indonesia, said he would "still rely on human or email communication" due to concerns about visa status implications from inaccurate guidance.

The district's chatbots run on Gravyty, formerly Ocelot, which uses manually maintained F.A.Q. libraries reviewed only once or twice yearly. Associate Vice Chancellor Betsy Regalado acknowledged that the current system cannot answer questions not in its library. She added the district plans to upgrade all nine colleges to a generative A.I. platform by late spring at no additional cost.

### QUICK HIT 1: Case Study

**Headline:** FSU Launches Nation's First AI Nursing Micro-Credential

**Source:** Florida State University News
**URL:** https://news.fsu.edu/news/health-medicine/2026/05/05/fsu-college-of-nursing-partners-with-chai-to-launch-nations-first-micro-credential-series-on-responsible-ai-for-nursing/

**Summary:**
Florida State University's College of Nursing has launched "Nursing Essentials of Responsible A.I.," the nation's first micro-credential series integrating artificial intelligence with nursing practice. The program was developed with the Coalition for Health A.I. and comprises seven micro-credentials covering foundational principles, data privacy, and practical applications across nursing workflows.

Dean Jing Wang stated that "nurses are at the heart of patient care, and as A.I. becomes an integral part of the healthcare team, they must have the tools to ensure its use is safe, ethical and human-centered." The courses are self-paced and designed for working healthcare professionals.

The initiative builds on F.S.U.'s 2024 launch of the nation's first Master of Science in Nursing focused on A.I. Applications in Healthcare and the establishment of the Nursing and Artificial Intelligence Innovation Consortium.

### QUICK HIT 2: AI Workforce & Careers

**Headline:** Yale: The Real Job Destruction from AI Is Hitting Before Careers Start

**Source:** Yale Insights
**URL:** https://insights.som.yale.edu/insights/the-real-job-destruction-from-ai-is-hitting-before-careers-can-start

**Summary:**
The real threat of A.I. job destruction may not be mass layoffs but fewer entry points into the workforce. Recent college graduate unemployment has climbed to nearly 6%, rising twice as fast as the broader workforce since 2022, Yale Insights reports.

A Federal Reserve Bank of New York study puts unemployment among computer science majors at 7.0% and computer engineering majors at 7.8% — rates comparable to anthropology, fine arts, and performing arts graduates. Software development job postings have fallen 53% from their late 2022 peak. Developer employment for workers ages 22 to 25 dropped nearly 20% from that same peak.

Companies report significant productivity gains from A.I. systems: banks cite 20-60% improvements and 30% faster turnaround; telecommunications firms achieved 60% reduction in manual operations. Rather than sudden mass layoffs, the mechanism is reduced hiring, extended employee tenure, and outsourced role elimination. Goldman Sachs estimates A.I. is reducing U.S. employment by roughly 16,000 jobs monthly.

### QUICK HIT 3: Investing in Innovation

**Headline:** Nvidia Board Member Donates $200M to USC for AI Research

**Source:** USC Today
**URL:** https://today.usc.edu/mark-and-mary-stevens-give-200-million-to-power-ai-research-across-usc/

**Summary:**
Nvidia board member Mark Stevens and his wife Mary have committed $200 million to advance artificial intelligence research at the University of Southern California — one of the largest gifts in the school's 146-year history.

The gift renames the School of Advanced Computing as the USC Mark and Mary Stevens School of Computing and Artificial Intelligence. The funding supports interdisciplinary research across health sciences, business, security, and the arts. It also backs a new bachelor's degree in artificial intelligence launching fall 2026, the A.I. for Business program, the Institute on Ethics and Trust in Computing, and the Center for A.I. in Society.

Stevens, worth more than $11 billion according to Forbes, was one of Nvidia's earliest backers in 1993 while at Sequoia Capital. He stated: "This is a key moment. I am confident that USC has the leadership and direction to run quickly."

### QUICK HIT 4: Insights & Trends

**Headline:** SUNY Sets Systemwide AI Policy for 64 Campuses

**Source:** Inside Higher Ed
**URL:** https://www.insidehighered.com/news/student-success/academic-life/2026/05/04/suny-sets-systemwide-ai-policy

**Summary:**
The State University of New York's Board of Trustees has approved a systemwide A.I. policy covering all 64 campuses. Each campus must adopt or update individual A.I. guidelines by December 31, 2026, with a one-time two-month extension available upon request.

Campus policies must define roles and responsibilities for faculty, staff, and students overseeing A.I. systems. They must add procurement safeguards to protect institutional data and prevent discriminatory outcomes. They must also account for differences across teaching, research, and administrative applications.

Chief Information Security Officer Jesse Sloman stated the policy aims to "augment what faculty are able to do" rather than replace educators, particularly through personalized tutoring and early-alert systems. Chancellor John B. King Jr. added the framework is "designed to expand the use of A.I. in ways that support students while maintaining oversight" and ensuring "accountability, transparency and appropriate safeguards."
