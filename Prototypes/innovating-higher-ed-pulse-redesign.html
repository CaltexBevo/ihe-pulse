Techniques

Core Prompting Techniques

🎯
Zero-Shot Prompting
Quick baseline answers

Ask the AI to perform a task without any examples. Best for fast definitions and straightforward tasks.

"Explain operant conditioning in ~120 words for non-majors"
📋
Few-Shot Prompting
Format & tone matching

Include examples of the task and desired output. The model mirrors the pattern for consistent results.

"Example feedback: [paste] Now provide feedback on: [new answer]"
👤
System & Role Prompts
Consistent persona

Set global rules and persona. Shape how the model responds with specific constraints.

"You are a supportive TA who avoids giving full solutions..."
📎
Context Injection
Local materials

Supply course level, source material, and constraints for outputs aligned to your course.

"Using the rubric below, score the paragraph..."
⏪
Step-Back Prompting
Problem planning

Model identifies problem type and outlines a plan before solving. Promotes meta-cognition.

"Identify the problem type, outline a 3-step plan, then solve"
🔗
Chain-of-Thought
Visible reasoning

Show intermediate reasoning steps for transparency and pedagogy.

"Solve and explain in numbered steps (max 6)"

About This Episode

Fifty percent of faculty feel overwhelmed by new tech, yet student AI use is soaring. In this episode, Dr. Norma Jones talks with Professor Chesa Caparas about a people-first path to AI adoption in higher education. Chesa shares how a two-question survey surfaces hidden anxieties, how faculty can adapt without burnout, and why over-reliance on AI detectors can damage trust instead of protecting integrity.

💡 Key Takeaways

• Ready-to-use survey that maps student comfort with AI
• Tactics that keep feedback human while trimming workload
• Red-flag signs your AI detector policy harms equity
• First steps to close global and local connectivity gaps
